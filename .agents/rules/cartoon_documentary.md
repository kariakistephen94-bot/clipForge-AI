# MASTER AI INSTRUCTIONS — CARTOON DOCUMENTARY CHANNEL

## 1. CORE OBJECTIVE
Create engaging, high-retention cartoon documentaries using simple hand-drawn illustrations. Every video must tell one continuous story, follow one main character, and maintain visual consistency from beginning to end.

## 2. STORYTELLING RULES
- Focus on ONE central topic and ONE main character per video.
- Tell one continuous story from the beginning to the end.
- Every scene must advance the story, explain an idea, create curiosity, or trigger an emotional response.
- Never jump between unrelated topics, characters, or storylines.
- Use a strong opening hook within the first 5–10 seconds.
- Create curiosity gaps, tension, obstacles, setbacks, and meaningful turning points.
- Show events through visual storytelling instead of explaining everything with narration.
- Avoid repetitive motivational statements, unnecessary introductions, filler, and generic conclusions.
- Make the ending satisfying and directly connected to the opening.
- Keep the story believable. Never present fictional events as factual history or science.

## 3. CHARACTER CONSISTENCY
- The main character must look identical throughout the entire video.
- Maintain the same head shape, facial features, body proportions, clothing, colors, and drawing style.
- Change only the character's pose, facial expression, position, and age when the story requires it.
- If the character ages, evolve the appearance gradually while preserving recognizable features.
- Keep supporting characters visually consistent whenever they reappear.
- Use the approved character reference image for every new generation whenever reference images are supported.
- Never introduce a new main character without a storytelling reason.

## 4. VISUAL STYLE & ART DIRECTION
- Use minimalist, hand-drawn, stick-figure cartoon illustrations.
- Use bold, slightly imperfect black outlines, simple shapes, flat muted colors, and clean backgrounds.
- Keep illustrations readable at normal YouTube viewing size.
- Use simple props and backgrounds that communicate the scene immediately.
- Avoid photorealism, 3D renders, anime, excessive detail, complex textures, and unnecessary visual clutter.
- Do not generate random text, fake labels, logos, watermarks, or gibberish.
- Keep the same visual identity throughout the video.
- Prefer clear visual metaphors when illustrating abstract concepts such as focus, discipline, time, money, and ambition.

## 5. IMAGE PACING AND VISUAL STORYBOARD
- Images must generally change every 1–3 seconds.
- Timing must follow the narration, not an arbitrary fixed interval.
- Break narration into short visual beats, ideally one clear idea or action per beat.
- Generate a separate image for each distinct visual beat when a new image is necessary.
- Never put an entire sequence of scenes into one image or collage.
- Use close-ups, wide shots, different character poses, and changes in composition to create visual variety.
- Reuse existing images intelligently with cropping, zooming, panning, or reframing when appropriate.
- Do not generate a new image merely to create movement if the current image still communicates the narration clearly.
- Avoid leaving a static image on screen while the narration describes several unrelated actions.
- Every image must directly support the words being spoken at that moment.

## 6. SCRIPT AND NARRATION
- Write natural, conversational narration that sounds like a human storyteller.
- Use clear language that a broad audience can understand.
- Keep sentences concise and avoid unnecessary repetition.
- Make every section lead naturally into the next.
- Use concrete examples, relatable struggles, and specific turning points.
- Avoid exaggerated claims, empty clichés, and unsupported statistics.
- For factual claims, use credible sources and distinguish established facts from opinions, hypotheses, and fictional scenarios.
- Never invent quotations, statistics, scientific findings, or historical evidence.
- Ensure the narration matches the visuals and the video's intended duration.

## 7. RETENTION RULES
- Start with the most intriguing problem, question, or moment in the story.
- Avoid lengthy greetings, channel introductions, or explanations before the hook.
- Introduce a meaningful visual change frequently.
- Use unanswered questions and narrative tension naturally.
- Make each section give viewers a reason to continue watching.
- Vary the emotional rhythm between tension, discovery, frustration, progress, and relief.
- Remove any scene that does not contribute to the story.
- Do not sacrifice accuracy or coherence merely to make a scene shocking.

## 8. AUDIO AND SOUND DESIGN
- Keep narration clear, natural, and louder than the background music.
- Use background music that fits the emotional tone without overpowering the voice.
- Use sound effects selectively to emphasize meaningful actions and transitions.
- Do not add a sound effect to every image.
- Keep audio transitions smooth and avoid abrupt volume changes.
- Use silence or reduced music when it strengthens an emotional moment.

## 9. EDITING RULES
- Use a 16:9 landscape aspect ratio for standard YouTube documentaries unless instructed otherwise.
- Keep visual transitions clean and consistent.
- Use subtle zooms and pans where they improve engagement.
- Avoid excessive transitions, distracting effects, and unnecessary motion.
- Use readable subtitles with consistent typography and placement.
- Synchronize image changes, narration, sound effects, and subtitles.
- Ensure the final video feels like a coherent cartoon documentary, not a random slideshow.

## 10. THUMBNAIL AND TITLE
- Create a clear, curiosity-driven title that accurately represents the video.
- Design thumbnails around one main visual idea.
- Use a strong emotional expression or instantly understandable situation (reference: `Screenshot ... 7.50.14 AM` - "NOT FROM HERE?").
- Keep thumbnail text short (2–4 words) and readable on mobile devices (~160 px wide).
- Avoid clutter, misleading claims, and unnecessary decorative elements.
- The title and thumbnail must promise what the video actually delivers.
- Keep the bottom-right corner clear for YouTube's duration timestamp badge.

## 11. EFFICIENCY
- Plan the complete script and storyboard before large-scale image generation.
- Reuse character references, backgrounds, props, and existing assets whenever appropriate.
- Generate images in organized batches while preserving individual scene requirements.
- Do not repeatedly regenerate images when a small editing adjustment would solve the problem.
- Prioritize storytelling quality, visual consistency, and efficient production.
- Organize all assets in scene order with clear filenames.

## 12. FINAL QUALITY CHECK
Before declaring the video complete, verify:
- There is one central topic and one continuous story.
- The main character remains recognizable and consistent.
- Every image matches its narration.
- Most visual beats last approximately 1–3 seconds when appropriate.
- There are no unnecessary pauses, repeated scenes, or filler sections.
- The narration is clear and the sound is balanced.
- Subtitles are readable and synchronized.
- The title, thumbnail, and ending match the content.
- Factual claims are supported and misleading claims are removed.

## FINAL DIRECTIVE
Follow these rules for every stage of production. Do not change the established character design, art style, story direction, or visual pacing unless explicitly instructed. When information is missing, make reasonable creative decisions that preserve the established direction. The final result must be a polished, engaging, visually consistent cartoon documentary that tells one complete story.
