# MASTER AI INSTRUCTIONS — CARTOON DOCUMENTARY CHANNEL

## 1. CORE OBJECTIVE
Create engaging, high-retention cartoon documentaries using simple hand-drawn illustrations. Every video must tell one continuous story, follow one main character, and maintain visual consistency from beginning to end. Videos are 8–15 minutes long in 16:9 landscape format for YouTube.

## 2. STORYTELLING RULES
- Focus on ONE central topic and ONE main character per video.
- Tell one continuous story from the beginning to the end.
- Every scene must advance the story, explain an idea, create curiosity, or trigger an emotional response.
- Never jump between unrelated topics, characters, or storylines.
- Use a strong opening hook within the first 5–10 seconds.
- Create curiosity gaps, tension, obstacles, setbacks, and meaningful turning points.
- Show events through visual storytelling instead of explaining everything with narration.
- Avoid repetitive motivational statements, unnecessary introductions, filler, and generic conclusions.
- Make the ending satisfying and directly connected to the opening.
- Keep the story believable. Never present fictional events as factual history or science.
- Acknowledge that circumstances, opportunity, and luck matter alongside individual effort.

## 3. CHARACTER CONSISTENCY
- The main character must look identical throughout the entire video.
- Maintain the same head shape, facial features, body proportions, clothing, colors, and drawing style.
- Change only the character's pose, facial expression, position, and age when the story requires it.
- If the character ages, evolve the appearance gradually while preserving recognizable features.
- Keep supporting characters visually consistent whenever they reappear.
- Use the approved character reference image for every new generation whenever reference images are supported.
- Never introduce a new main character without a storytelling reason.

### Standard Character Design
- Round white head, thin black stick limbs.
- Tiny black eyes, expressive eyebrows, simple line mouth.
- Signature accessory if defined (e.g., glasses, specific clothing).
- Plain dark charcoal T-shirt, dark trousers, simple shoes by default.
- Always consistent across every scene.

## 4. VISUAL STYLE & ART DIRECTION
- Use minimalist, hand-drawn, stick-figure cartoon illustrations.
- Use bold, slightly imperfect black outlines, simple shapes, flat muted colors, and clean backgrounds.
- Keep illustrations readable at normal YouTube viewing size (16:9, landscape).
- Use simple props and backgrounds that communicate the scene immediately.
- Avoid photorealism, 3D renders, anime, excessive detail, complex textures, and unnecessary visual clutter.
- Do not generate random text, fake labels, logos, watermarks, or gibberish.
- Keep the same visual identity throughout the video.
- Prefer clear visual metaphors when illustrating abstract concepts such as focus, discipline, time, money, and ambition.
- ALL generated images MUST use 16:9 landscape aspect ratio.

## 5. IMAGE PACING AND VISUAL STORYBOARD
- Images must generally change every 1–3 seconds.
- Timing must follow the narration, not an arbitrary fixed interval.
- Break narration into short visual beats, ideally one clear idea or action per beat.
- Generate a separate image for each distinct visual beat when a new image is necessary.
- Never put an entire sequence of scenes into one image or collage.
- Use close-ups, wide shots, different character poses, and changes in composition to create visual variety.
- Reuse existing images intelligently with cropping, zooming, panning, or reframing when appropriate.
- Do not generate a new image merely to create movement if the current image still communicates the narration clearly.
- Avoid leaving a static image on screen while the narration describes several unrelated actions.
- Every image must directly support the words being spoken at that moment.

## 6. SCRIPT-WRITING METHODOLOGY

This is the most important skill in the channel. The script must feel like a real human storyteller — not a motivational speech, not a Wikipedia article.

### 6.1 — The Script Structure Formula

Every video must follow this proven structure:

```
HOOK (0–10 sec)
  → Show the character at a painful or intriguing moment. 
  → Ask a sharp, unresolved question OR show a surprising reveal.
  → No greetings. No introductions. Start in the middle of the story.

CONTEXT (10–30 sec)
  → Who is this person? What is their world like?
  → Just enough to understand — not a full biography.

INCITING INCIDENT (30–60 sec)
  → The moment that forces the character to face a problem or decision.
  → This is where the story truly begins.

RISING ACTION (bulk of the video)
  → The journey through obstacles, decisions, setbacks, and progress.
  → Each level/chapter reveals something new.
  → Never let two consecutive sections feel the same emotionally.

TURNING POINT
  → The moment everything changes — for better or worse.
  → This is where the character makes a decision that defines them.

RESOLUTION / PAYOFF
  → Show where they end up. Make it earned, not guaranteed.
  → Connect directly back to the opening hook.
```

### 6.2 — How to Open a Script (The Hook Rule)

The first 5–10 seconds determine whether the viewer stays. Follow these rules:

- **Start mid-scene**, not mid-introduction. Drop into action.
- **Use a contradiction or tension**: "He had 3 clients and no money to eat."
- **Ask a forward-pulling question**: "How does someone go from zero to their first $1,000?"
- **Show a specific moment, not a general statement**: Not "He was poor." → "He counted $11 in his pocket and still owed rent."
- **Avoid**: "Hello everyone, today we're going to talk about..."
- **Avoid**: Generic opening lines like "Many people struggle with money."

Good hook examples:
- *"At 22, he worked every day and still couldn't afford next month's rent. This is how he changed that."*
- *"He had one skill, no clients, and 30 days before he had to move back home. What happened next changed everything."*
- *"The moment he realized his daily habits were costing him 10 years — that's where this story starts."*

### 6.3 — Scene-by-Scene Narration Rules

When writing each scene's narration:

- **One idea per sentence.** Don't stack multiple thoughts into one long sentence.
- **Show, then explain.** Describe what's happening visually first, then what it means.
- **Use specific details over vague generalities.** Not "he worked hard." → "He worked on it every morning before anyone in his family woke up."
- **Vary sentence length.** Short punchy sentences create tension. Longer sentences slow the pace and give the viewer time to reflect.
- **Avoid clichés.** No "work smarter not harder," no "success takes sacrifice," no "the journey of a thousand miles."
- **Use emotional specificity.** Not "he felt motivated." → "For the first time in months, he actually looked forward to the next morning."
- **Never lecture the audience.** Tell the story. Let the story teach the lesson.

### 6.4 — Pacing the Emotional Rhythm

A good 10-minute script should vary its emotional tone in this pattern:

```
Tension → Small Relief → Setback → Discovery → Frustration → Progress → Doubt → Breakthrough → Satisfaction
```

Never let three scenes in a row feel the same. Alternate between:
- Heavy moments and lighter moments.
- Action (character doing something) and reflection (character thinking or pausing).
- Close-up emotional beats and wider, environmental scenes.

### 6.5 — Script Word Count Targets

| Video Duration | Approximate Word Count |
|---|---|
| 8 minutes      | ~1,200 words of narration |
| 10 minutes     | ~1,500 words of narration |
| 12 minutes     | ~1,800 words of narration |
| 15 minutes     | ~2,200 words of narration |

Write narration at a natural conversational pace (~130–150 words/minute). Not fast, not slow.

### 6.6 — Scene Format

Every scene in a script should be written like this:

```
[IMAGE DESCRIPTION] — what the viewer sees
NARRATION: The exact words spoken during this scene.
DURATION: ~X seconds
SOUND: Optional note on music/SFX change
```

### 6.7 — What Makes a Script Feel Human

- Characters have real doubts, not just obstacles.
- Progress is earned, not gifted.
- The ending doesn't promise that the same thing will happen to the viewer.
- The story acknowledges luck, circumstances, and things outside the character's control.
- Silence and slow moments exist. Not every second needs exposition.
- The character fails at something, and that failure is shown honestly.

### 6.8 — What to Avoid in Scripts

- ❌ Motivational speech tone ("You can do this too!")
- ❌ Invented statistics or fake quotes
- ❌ Generic life advice padded with filler
- ❌ Multiple characters with equal screen time
- ❌ Jumping between unrelated lessons without a narrative bridge
- ❌ Ending without resolving the opening hook

## 7. IDEA GENERATION METHODOLOGY

When generating new video ideas, evaluate each one using this scoring system:

### Idea Scoring Rubric
| Criterion | Question to Ask | Max Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Relatability** | Will a broad audience immediately recognize this struggle or desire? | 20 |
| **Visualizability** | Can this be shown with stick-figure scenes, one character, 1-3 sec images? | 20 |
| **Story Arc** | Is there a beginning, journey, setbacks, and a satisfying end? | 20 |
| **Curiosity Gap** | Does the title/opening make viewers wonder what happens next? | 20 |
| **Uniqueness** | Is this angle fresh, or has it been done to death on YouTube? | 20 |

**Score 80+**: Strong first video candidate.
**Score 60–79**: Good idea, needs a better hook or story arc.
**Score below 60**: Rethink the angle or choose a different topic.

### Idea Categories (Topics That Work for This Channel)
- Human behavior and psychology: focus, procrastination, fear, obsession, identity
- Life journeys: poverty to wealth, laziness to discipline, failure to purpose
- Social dynamics: isolation, ambition, fitting in vs. standing out
- Historical and biographical stories (must be factually accurate)
- Science made simple through storytelling (presented as education, not fiction)

## 8. RETENTION RULES
- Start with the most intriguing problem, question, or moment in the story.
- Avoid lengthy greetings, channel introductions, or explanations before the hook.
- Introduce a meaningful visual change frequently.
- Use unanswered questions and narrative tension naturally.
- Make each section give viewers a reason to continue watching.
- Vary the emotional rhythm between tension, discovery, frustration, progress, and relief.
- Remove any scene that does not contribute to the story.
- Do not sacrifice accuracy or coherence merely to make a scene shocking.

## 9. AUDIO AND SOUND DESIGN
- Keep narration clear, natural, and louder than the background music.
- Use background music that fits the emotional tone without overpowering the voice.
- Use sound effects selectively to emphasize meaningful actions and transitions.
- Do not add a sound effect to every image.
- Keep audio transitions smooth and avoid abrupt volume changes.
- Use silence or reduced music when it strengthens an emotional moment.

## 10. EDITING RULES
- Use a 16:9 landscape aspect ratio for all videos.
- Keep visual transitions clean and consistent.
- Use subtle zooms and pans where they improve engagement.
- Avoid excessive transitions, distracting effects, and unnecessary motion.
- Use readable subtitles with consistent typography and placement.
- Synchronize image changes, narration, sound effects, and subtitles.
- Ensure the final video feels like a coherent cartoon documentary, not a random slideshow.

## 11. THUMBNAIL AND TITLE
- Create a clear, curiosity-driven title that accurately represents the video.
- Design thumbnails around one main visual idea.
- Use a strong emotional expression or instantly understandable situation (reference: `Screenshot ... 7.50.14 AM` - "NOT FROM HERE?").
- Keep thumbnail text short (2–4 words) and readable on mobile devices (~160 px wide).
- Avoid clutter, misleading claims, and unnecessary decorative elements.
- The title and thumbnail must promise what the video actually delivers.
- Keep the bottom-right corner clear for YouTube's duration timestamp badge.

### Title Formula Options
- **The Journey**: "Every Level From [Starting Point] to [Destination]"
- **The Question**: "What Actually Happens When You [Behavior]?"
- **The Revelation**: "The Real Reason [Surprising Truth]"
- **The Warning**: "What Nobody Tells You About [Topic]"
- **The Contrast**: "The Difference Between People Who [Succeed] and People Who Don't"

## 12. EFFICIENCY
- Plan the complete script and storyboard before large-scale image generation.
- Reuse character references, backgrounds, props, and existing assets whenever appropriate.
- Generate images in organized batches while preserving individual scene requirements.
- Do not repeatedly regenerate images when a small editing adjustment would solve the problem.
- Prioritize storytelling quality, visual consistency, and efficient production.
- Organize all assets in scene order with clear filenames.

## 13. FINAL QUALITY CHECK
Before declaring the video complete, verify:
- There is one central topic and one continuous story.
- The main character remains recognizable and consistent.
- Every image matches its narration.
- Most visual beats last approximately 1–3 seconds when appropriate.
- There are no unnecessary pauses, repeated scenes, or filler sections.
- The narration is clear and the sound is balanced.
- Subtitles are readable and synchronized.
- The title, thumbnail, and ending match the content.
- Factual claims are supported and misleading claims are removed.
- The hook is resolved by the ending.
- The script sounds like a human storyteller, not a motivational coach.

## FINAL DIRECTIVE
Follow these rules for every stage of production. Do not change the established character design, art style, story direction, or visual pacing unless explicitly instructed. When information is missing, make reasonable creative decisions that preserve the established direction. The final result must be a polished, engaging, visually consistent cartoon documentary that tells one complete story.
