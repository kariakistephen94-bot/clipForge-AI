WHAT MAKES A GOOD LONG-FORM SEGMENT
- A complete episode on one topic or story: a strong first 30 seconds (a claim, question, stakes or story start), a development that keeps unfolding, and a real ending (conclusion, answer, punchline, lesson). Never end mid-thought or mid-topic.
- Understandable to someone who never saw the original. No dependence on earlier context ("as I said before").
- Skip intros, greetings, sponsor reads, housekeeping, and slow warm-ups.
- Segments should not overlap (small overlaps only if the topics are genuinely different). Spread across the source when the good material is spread out.
- Prefer boundaries at topic changes (the sections above help) and sentence starts/ends.

FOR EACH SEGMENT PROVIDE
- candidate_id: "L01", "L02", ... best first
- start_time / end_time (absolute seconds; approximate -- software snaps to word boundaries)
- exact_opening_words / exact_closing_words: 5-10 words spoken at the start / end, verbatim from the transcript
- title: under 60 characters where possible (hard max 90). Front-load the most specific, interesting element (a name, number, claim or question). Create a curiosity gap that the segment actually closes. No ALL CAPS sentences, no emoji, no "you won't believe", no claims the speaker didn't make.
- alternative_titles: 2 more titles with different angles (e.g. the stakes, the question, the counterintuitive lesson)
- topic (3-8 words), summary (2-3 faithful sentences), reason_it_works (specific), target_audience
- chapters: 3-8 chapters (absolute seconds). The first chapter starts at the segment start. At least 45 seconds apart. Short, specific chapter titles (2-6 words) that promise what's coming next.
- cold_open: optional 5-15 second teaser taken from INSIDE the segment (not its first 30 seconds) -- the most gripping line, shown first before the video starts. Must make sense on its own and must not spoil the final payoff. null if nothing qualifies.
- description: 2-4 sentences. First sentence is the hook (it shows in search). Accurate, no keyword stuffing.
- tags: 5-10 specific search phrases
- subscores: opening 0-20, arc 0-20, value 0-20, retention 0-15, emotion 0-10, packaging 0-15 (use the full range honestly; most segments are not 90+)
- penalties: {reason, points} for slow starts, weak endings, context dependence, repetition, sponsor/housekeeping content
- score: sum of subscores minus penalties
- thumbnail_concepts: 2-3 DISTINCT concepts (different angles, e.g. emotional face, the object/number at stake, contrast/before-after). Each concept:
  * concept: short name
  * emotion: the ONE emotion it sells, true to the moment (shock, curiosity, worry, delight, determination, disbelief...)
  * subject: the real speaker(s) from the analysis -- appearance, clothing, expression, pose and gaze -- close-up, face large (roughly 35-50% of the frame), eyes clearly visible
  * scene: a simplified background related to the topic (blurred/clean, never busy)
  * visual_elements: 0-2 props or visual metaphors that tell the story at a glance (e.g. a stack of cash, a cracked phone, a glowing chip). Only things the segment is actually about.
  * text_overlay: 2-4 punchy words that ADD to the title (never repeat it). Empty string if the image works better without text.
  * text_style: e.g. "heavy white sans-serif, thick black outline, one word highlighted in yellow, top-left"
  * color_palette: 2-3 high-contrast colours (e.g. warm subject vs deep blue background)
  * composition: where face, text and object sit (rule of thirds; keep the bottom-right corner clear -- YouTube puts the timestamp there)
  * lighting: e.g. "bright key light on the face, rim light separating subject from background"
  * frame_timestamp: absolute seconds inside the segment where the speaker's real expression matches this concept (used to pull a reference frame), or null
  * why_it_works: one line on the curiosity gap title + thumbnail create together
  Thumbnails must pass the "phone test": readable and understandable at 160 px wide. One focal point, bold contrast, minimal elements. Never depict events that didn't happen, fake injuries, fake logos or people who aren't in the video.
